# Day 23 — 7 February 1984

*Gold day, draft 1. Moselle coal basin, a miners' estate outside Freyming-Merlebach. Thaw and fog. Nine and a half hours of light. Hold: Frayed.*

## Dawn

He had slept in the lamp room at the pithead among the racks where the miners had hung their lamps and the racks were empty. In the night the frost had given. Now water ran in the gutters and dripped from the headframe somewhere above him in the fog.

He was hungry in the way that had become ordinary. The cough was no worse. His feet had been wet since the day before and he took off his boots and his socks and wrung the socks out and put them on again because there were no others.

## Plan

The fog lay on the coal basin so that he could see a hundred yards and no more. Below the pithead a road ran down between the slag heaps and at the gate a sign said Freyming-Merlebach 3.

> 1. Stay in the lamp room and dry out.
> 2. **Follow the road down toward the town.**
> 3. Climb the slag heap and look.

## Move

The slag heaps stood up out of the fog black and steep and without a blade of grass on them and one of them was burning somewhere inside itself. Smoke came out of its flank in a thin seam and the snow around the seam had gone and the ground there was dry and warm. He held his hands to it a while.

On the road a Simca sat on its rims with the paint burned off it to the bare metal. He did not look inside.

At the roundabout someone had written on the wall of a bakery in tar, the letters a foot high.

ICI ON TIRE SUR LES PILLARDS

He did not know pillards. He knew enough of the rest.

## Inspect

A cité of the mining company. Rows of brick houses built in pairs, each with its garden and its shed and a number painted over the door, the same house again and again down the street until the fog took it. Most of the shutters were closed. From one chimney halfway along came a thread of coal smoke that smelled of sulphur, and in that garden washing hung frozen on a line.

> 1. The house with the smoke.
> 2. **An empty house at the end of the row.**
> 3. The shop on the corner.

## Loot

The door had been kicked in before him. The drawers were out on the floor and the mattresses slit and the bread bin open and empty. In the cellar he found half a sack of coal briquettes and the stub of a candle.

On the kitchen table a newspaper lay under a cup. Le Républicain Lorrain. Samedi 31 décembre 1983. The headline ran the width of the page.

LA FRANCE NE SERA PAS ENTRAÎNÉE, AFFIRME LE PRÉSIDENT

He understood France and he understood président. Under it was a photograph of a bridge broken in the middle and its two ends hanging into a river and soldiers on the bank looking at it. He could not tell whose soldiers. He folded the paper into his smock.

When he came out a man was standing in the next garden with a pick handle. A thickset man with a moustache and a cloth cap and a miner's blue scars on the backs of his hands.

Qu'est-ce que tu fais là, the man said.

He held his hands out from his sides. Anglais, he said.

The man called something over his shoulder in a language that was not French. Gel buraya. Çabuk. A girl came to the door of the house in a coat too big for her and stood behind him.

> 1. **Ask for English.**
> 2. Offer the pear spirit.
> 3. Back away down the street.

English, he said. Do you speak English.

The man shook his head. The girl looked at her father and then at him.

I speak a little, she said. From school.

I need water.

She spoke to her father and he answered her. There is no water, she said. The pump is broken.

He looked past them at the pump in their yard. Cast iron, green paint, a curved handle hanging down.

I can mend that, he said.

## Tend

The handle pin had rusted in and he worked it out with the point of the bayonet and drew the rod up out of the barrel. The leather cup on the plunger had dried and split in the frost. The man watched him and then went into the shed and came back with an old belt and held it out.

He cut a circle from the belt and pared it and soaked it in a pan of water the girl brought and pressed it to shape round the plunger with his thumbs. The two fingers on his left hand were no use to him and he did it with the others. He set the rod back and drove in the pin and poured the pan into the top to prime it and worked the handle. Nothing. He worked it again. On the fourth stroke water came out of the spout in a gout and then steadily and ran grey and then clear.

The man said a word he did not know and put a hand on his shoulder and took it away.

They gave him tea in a small glass shaped like a tulip and flat bread folded round a crumble of white cheese. He ate in the doorway because he did not want to go in and they did not ask him. He filled his water bottle and the three Vittel bottles at the pump.

Where you go, the girl said.

England.

She told her father and her father said something short and looked at the ground.

He says England is finish, she said. Everybody says it.

He drank the rest of the tea and gave her back the glass.

## Night

> 1. **Sleep in the empty house.**
> 2. Ask to sleep in their shed.
> 3. Walk on in the dark.

He lit the briquettes in the grate of the empty house and the smoke went up among the other smoke. He lay on the slit mattress with the rifle beside him.

Through the wall he could hear them. Not the words. A man's voice saying the same few phrases over low and the creak of the floor as he knelt and stood and knelt again, and then quiet.

## Journal

7 Feb. Merlebach. Fixed a pump for a Turkish family, the girl had some English. Tea. They say England is finished. They dont know that. Paper from 31 Dec says France is keeping out of it.
