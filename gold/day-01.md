# Day 1 — 16 January 1984

*Gold day, draft 1. Pays de Bitche, the cabin to the forester's house. Overcast, still, hard frost. Eight hours of light.*

## Dawn

He woke before the light and lay listening. The stove had gone out in the night and the cold had come down from the roof and got into the blankets with him. He coughed and lay still and coughed again and spat into the dark.

When he could make out the rafters he sat up and pulled on his boots. The two fingers of his left hand would not take the laces and he tied them with his right hand and his teeth.

There were twenty-six notches cut in the doorframe with the bayonet. He counted them and counted them again. He did not remember cutting the last few.

He drank from the water bottle and shook it. There was not much left.

## Plan

He lifted the bar and opened the door.

The clearing lay under a skin of snow the grey of a slate roof and the trees stood round it and did not move. Over the ridge the sun hung in the overcast like a lamp behind a blind and he could look straight at it without narrowing his eyes.

There was no road noise. No saw. No bird.

He stood in the doorway a long time with the rifle and then he went back in and began to pack.

> 1. Stay another day by the stove.
> 2. **Take the logging track down toward the valley.**
> 3. Climb the ridge and look north.

## Move

The track went down through the firs in long bends with the snow unmarked on it. Timber lay stacked at the side in cords with numbers painted on the ends in blue. Under the snow the ruts were frozen hard and he walked in them.

At the second bend a forestry skidder stood with its blade down and the cab door open. He stopped and looked at it the way he would have looked at it in the yard at home. The battery would be flat. The diesel would have waxed in the lines with the cold and it would want warming before it ran and there was nothing to warm it with. He shut the cab door and went on.

Lower down where the firs gave way to beech a jay lay on the track on its back with its feet drawn up. Further on another. He stepped round them.

By noon he could see a roof below him through the trees.

## Inspect

A stone house at the edge of the forest with a slate roof and green shutters and a sign on the gatepost. Maison Forestière. Office National des Forêts. A woodshed with a cord of beech under it. A kennel with a chain running out of it into the snow and nothing on the end of the chain. No smoke from the chimney. No tracks to the door but the ones he made.

> 1. **The house.**
> 2. The woodshed.
> 3. The kennel.

## Loot

A sheet of paper was pinned to the door. Pencil, in a careful hand.

Partis chez ma sœur à Bitche. 3 janvier. Laissez la maison en ordre. J.-M. Weber, garde forestier.

He read it twice. He understood partis and he understood Bitche and the date. He took the pin out and folded the paper and put it in his smock.

The front door was locked and he went round to the back and the back door was not. The kitchen had been left clean. The chairs pushed in. The plates in the rack. Whoever had gone had gone in their own time and had meant to come back.

He took what there was. Four candles in a drawer. A box of kitchen matches. Under the sink a cardboard tray of Vittel bottles, five whole and one split by the frost. On a shelf a bottle of clear spirit with a whole pear standing in it. By the door in a frame a forestry map of the domaine with the parcels numbered and the tracks marked in red, and he took it down and knocked the glass out on the edge of the table and rolled the map and put it in his pack.

## Tend

He lit the kitchen stove with beech from the shed and sat close to it with his coat open. He heated half the tin of bacon grill in the mess tin and ate it with the bayonet and drank a bottle of the water without stopping. The other half he covered with the lid and set on the sill.

He held his left hand to the heat and worked it. The two fingers had set crooked and they would bend so far and no further.

From the inside pocket of the smock he took the letter and unfolded it.

There it stopped. He found the pencil and held it over the page a while. Then he folded the letter along its creases and put it back.

## Night

> 1. **Sleep in the house by the stove.**
> 2. Sleep in the woodshed without a fire.

He closed the shutters and wedged a chair under the handle of the back door and banked the stove. Before he lay down he went out into the yard to piss and stood afterward looking north over the trees.

The sky there was not dark. Along the rim of the hills a long low band of red lay under the cloud and did not move and did not fade, as if some country beyond them had been left burning with no one remaining to put it out.

Somewhere down the valley a dog barked and another answered and then they stopped.

He went in and lay on the floor by the stove in his coat with the rifle along his side and pinched out the candle and slept.

## Journal

16 Jan. Left the cabin. Walked down to a foresters house, nobody there. Note on the door says they went to Bitche on the 3rd. Must be a centre set up there, Red Cross or the army. Will make for Bitche. Found a map. Fingers no better. Sky red to the north all night.
