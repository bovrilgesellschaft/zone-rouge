# Day 41 — 25 February 1984

*Gold day, draft 1. Argonne forest, a German blockhouse from the first war. Sleet. Ten hours of light, none of it walked. Hold: Unmoored. Left calf cut on old wire three days ago, now infected. Fever.*

## Dawn

He woke with his shirt wet through and his teeth going. Grey light in the doorway and the sleet hissing in the dead leaves outside. For a while he did not know where he was and he lay and looked at the concrete over him and the rusted rails set in it and the letters someone had cut there seventy years ago, and then he knew.

There had been a rattle in the night, or a bell. He could not say which.

His left leg was hot from the knee down. He did not look at it yet.

## Plan

He got up on one knee and then onto his feet and the doorway went away from him and came back. He sat down against the wall.

> 1. **Stay and tend the leg.**
> 2. Walk on toward the Aisne.

## Tend

He unwound the strip of pillowcase from his calf. It had stuck and he wet it from the bottle and drew it off slowly. The cut was the length of his finger and the edges had swollen and turned up and a yellow crust lay in it. From the top of it a thin red line ran up the inside of his leg toward the knee. He looked at the line a long time.

He lit the last hexamine block and boiled water in the mess tin and let it cool a little and washed the cut with it and picked the crust out with the tip of the bayonet held in the flame. Then he uncorked the pear spirit and poured what was left of it into the wound. He sat with his back against the concrete and his eyes shut and his jaw set until it passed.

He boiled the strip of pillowcase and wrung it out and bound the leg again.

He set his food out on the floor in a row. A tin of sardines. Two tins with no labels. The heel of a loaf gone hard. He looked at it and put the four tins back in the pack.

Later he took the photograph from his breast pocket and held it to the light from the door. It was a photograph of his wife and daughter by the sea. He could not remember what she was laughing at. He held it until his hand began to shake and then he put it away.

When the light began to go he saw a lamp on the hill across the ravine. Low among the trees and steady. It did not move along the hill and it did not come down. He watched it until he could not tell whether he saw it still or only the place where it had been.

## Night

> 1. **Light a fire in the blockhouse.**
> 2. No fire.

He broke dead branches across his knee and built the fire inside the doorway where the smoke would go out and the light would not. It caught and he lay down beside it with the rifle.

The fire burned down and he fed it and it burned down again. The rattle was going somewhere along the bank and someone was shouting the word for gas and he had the respirator on and the eyepieces were fogging. There was no smell. There was nothing on the air at all. A hand had hold of his sleeve, or he had hold of it, and someone said his name close to him through the rubber, and there was a weight on him and then there was not. The rattle went on. The river went under the bridge in the dark the way it always had.

When he looked again the fire was embers and the sleet had stopped. His right hand was closed so hard on the cuff of his own sleeve that he had to open the fingers one at a time with the other hand.

## Journal

25 Feb. Leg bad. Stayed put. Spirit gone. 4 tins. Somebody with a lamp up on the hill opposite, didnt come down.
